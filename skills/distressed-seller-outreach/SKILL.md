# Distressed & Motivated Seller Outreach

Cold outreach to people in financial distress or pre-foreclosure is the highest-legal-risk, highest-harm-potential content this repo drafts. This skill assumes the user is a **licensed real estate agent offering to represent and list the property** - not an unlicensed investor soliciting to buy the equity directly. That distinction matters: agent-to-homeowner "let me help you sell this" outreach is normal, legal solicitation; "I'll buy your house directly for cash" outreach to someone in foreclosure is what most state foreclosure-consultant / equity-purchaser statutes exist to regulate (mandatory written disclosures, specific contract language, rescission periods, licensing/bonding requirements). If the user says they're actually doing the latter, say so explicitly and point them to their state's law and their broker/counsel before drafting anything - don't just draft it anyway.

## Before drafting anything (compliance gate - don't skip this)

Ask, or use what the user already told you, to confirm:

1. **Where did this list come from, and is there consent to text?** A cold text to a stranger's cell phone generally needs prior express consent under the TCPA. A purchased/scraped pre-foreclosure (NOD/NTS) list does **not** come with texting consent attached just because the filing is public record. If there's no consent, recommend mail (a postcard/letter has no TCPA consent requirement) or a manual, individually-placed phone call instead of a text blast - and say so plainly rather than drafting texts for a list that shouldn't be texted.
2. **Do Not Call.** If this is going to be called (not just texted), check the number against the National DNC Registry and any state-specific list before dialing.
3. **State foreclosure-consultant / equity-purchaser law**, for pre-foreclosure leads specifically. Many states (California Civil Code § 2945 et seq., Colorado's Foreclosure Protection Act, Florida § 501.1377, New York RPL § 265-a, and others) impose specific disclosure and contract requirements on anyone soliciting a homeowner in foreclosure - even more so for anyone offering to buy rather than list. This skill is not a substitute for checking the user's specific state's law with their broker or counsel; say that explicitly rather than implying the scripts below are pre-cleared for every state.
4. **Never conceal or misrepresent the reason for contact.** Some outreach guidance circulating in the industry advises never mentioning foreclosure directly, to avoid the recipient hanging up. Don't follow that advice - concealing why you're contacting someone in this situation is close to the deceptive-solicitation conduct these statutes exist to prevent, and it's the opposite of the trust an agent needs to actually help someone. Identify yourself, your brokerage, and the real reason for the message in the first line, every time.
5. **CAN-SPAM, for email.** Real "From" name, non-deceptive subject line, a physical mailing address in the footer, and a working opt-out honored within 10 business days.

If any of this can't be confirmed, say so and offer the compliant alternative (mail, a manually-placed call, or simply not contacting that list) instead of drafting the outreach anyway.

## Lead categories

Each category gets a different opening because the situation is different - a probate sale and a foreclosure are not the same conversation, even though both might route to "motivated seller" in the pipeline.

- **Pre-foreclosure** - a Notice of Default/Notice of Trustee Sale is public record; assume the homeowner already knows their situation and is likely fielding other outreach. Lead with empathy and a real alternative (sell before the auction date, keep some equity), not urgency-manufacturing.
- **General financial distress** (self-identified - job loss, medical, etc., not from a NOD list) - softer, since there's no public-record trigger to reference; anchor to whatever prompted the lead (an ad response, a form fill) instead.
- **Probate / inherited property** - not financial distress in the same sense, but often an unwanted, unfamiliar asset with real emotional weight (a death in the family). Practical and low-pressure tone.
- **Divorce** - sensitive, often two decision-makers, avoid anything that could read as taking a side.
- **Landlord burnout** - the most transactional of the group; lean into the practical pain (vacancy, repairs, difficult tenants) rather than empathy-for-hardship framing.

## Script structure

**Texts**: under 320 characters. Identify yourself and your brokerage by name in the first message - never lead with just "Hi, quick question" and no identification. One clear ask. Always include an opt-out ("reply STOP to opt out"). No countdown timers, no fake urgency, no ALL CAPS.

**Emails**: under ~150 words, one CTA, honest subject line (not "Re:" on a message that isn't a reply, not implying the sender is the bank/court/government). Footer needs a physical mailing address and unsubscribe link per CAN-SPAM.

### Pre-foreclosure

Text 1: `Hi {firstName}, this is {agentName} with {brokerage} - a licensed local agent, not a lender or the bank. I help homeowners who are weighing their options before a sale date. No pressure, just wanted to make sure you know selling is one of them. Worth a 5-min call? Reply STOP to opt out.`

Text 2 (no reply to first): `Hi {firstName}, following up - totally understand if the timing's not right. If it'd help to just know what your home could sell for and how fast, happy to send that over, no obligation. {agentName}`

Email subject: `A straightforward option before your sale date`
Email body:
```
Hi {firstName},

My name is {agentName}, a licensed real estate agent with {brokerage} here in {area}. I work with homeowners who are navigating a difficult mortgage situation, and wanted to reach out directly rather than have you find out about this option too late.

Selling before the scheduled sale date can, in some cases, let you walk away with equity instead of losing it - it depends on your specific numbers, which I'd be glad to look at with you, no cost or obligation.

If it would help to talk through what your options actually look like, I'm available at {phone} or you can just reply to this email.

{agentName}
{brokerage}
{phone}
{mailing address}
[Unsubscribe]
```

### General financial distress

Text: `Hi {firstName}, this is {agentName} with {brokerage}. Saw you looked into selling options recently - wanted to reach out directly in case now's a hard time and a faster sale timeline would help. No pressure either way. Reply STOP to opt out.`

Email subject: `Following up on your home sale question`
Email body:
```
Hi {firstName},

This is {agentName} with {brokerage}. I saw you'd looked into selling, and wanted to check in directly rather than let it sit - sometimes the right timeline matters as much as the right price.

Happy to put together a no-obligation look at what your home could sell for and how quickly, on your schedule. Just reply here or call/text {phone}.

{agentName}
{brokerage}
{phone}
{mailing address}
[Unsubscribe]
```

### Probate / inherited property

Text: `Hi {firstName}, this is {agentName} with {brokerage}. I help families sort out what to do with a property they've inherited - sell, rent, or hold. No agenda, just happy to walk through the options if useful. Reply STOP to opt out.`

Email subject: `Options for the property at {propertyAddress}`
Email body:
```
Hi {firstName},

My name is {agentName}, a local agent with {brokerage}. I work with families handling an inherited property, and know that's often the last thing anyone wants to deal with alongside everything else.

If it would help, I'm glad to put together a no-cost estimate of what the property could sell for as-is, and walk through what selling, renting, or holding onto it would each actually look like - whichever direction makes sense for your family.

No pressure at all - reach out whenever is right for you.

{agentName}
{brokerage}
{phone}
{mailing address}
[Unsubscribe]
```

### Divorce

Text: `Hi {firstName}, this is {agentName} with {brokerage}. I help people sort out a home sale during a separation - keeping things fair, straightforward, and low-stress for everyone involved. Happy to answer questions, no pressure. Reply STOP to opt out.`

Email subject: `A neutral option for the home, if it's helpful`
Email body:
```
Hi {firstName},

This is {agentName} with {brokerage}. I work with people navigating a home sale during a separation, and understand that clarity and fairness matter more than a fast pitch right now.

If it would help to have a no-obligation valuation and a straightforward plan you both can look at, I'm glad to put that together - happy to loop in both parties directly if that's easier.

{agentName}
{brokerage}
{phone}
{mailing address}
[Unsubscribe]
```

### Landlord burnout

Text: `Hi {firstName}, this is {agentName} with {brokerage}. Curious if you've thought about selling {propertyAddress} instead of dealing with another tenant turnover/repair cycle - happy to run the numbers either way. Reply STOP to opt out.`

Email subject: `What {propertyAddress} could sell for vs. renting it another year`
Email body:
```
Hi {firstName},

This is {agentName} with {brokerage}. A lot of landlords I work with reach a point where the vacancy/repair/tenant cycle costs more than it's worth - wanted to check whether that's on your radar for {propertyAddress}.

Happy to run a quick comparison: what it'd sell for now vs. another year of renting, no obligation either way.

{agentName}
{brokerage}
{phone}
{mailing address}
[Unsubscribe]
```

## Workflow

1. **Run the compliance gate above first**, out loud - don't silently assume consent/list-source is fine.
2. **Read `BRAND.md`** for voice/tone and adjust the drafts above to match, rather than sending them verbatim - these are starting templates, not a script to read robotically.
3. **Confirm which categor(ies)** apply to this batch, and get the placeholder values ({propertyAddress}, source of the lead, etc.).
4. **Draft the text + email pair(s)**, filled in.
5. **Route every batch through `notify_slack` for human review before anything sends** (see `skills/slack-review-queue`) - unlike `speed-to-lead` (which reacts to a lead who already opted in via a form), this is cold outreach to people who haven't engaged yet, and it deserves a human read every time, no exceptions for this skill. **Never call `send_email` directly from this skill**, even though the tool exists (see the README's "Email setup" section) and even if asked to "just send it" - draft it, route it to Slack, and let the human send it themselves once approved.
6. **After approval and if the lead exists in FUB**, offer to log it via `add_lead`/`add_note` tagged by category (e.g. `"Pre-Foreclosure"`, `"Probate"`) so responses and follow-up history are tracked centrally, the same way `fub-lead-scoring` expects.

## What this skill deliberately does not do

- It does not draft "cash offer" / equity-purchase scripts for someone acting as a buyer rather than a listing agent - that's a different legal category (see the compliance gate) and isn't covered here.
- It does not check TCPA consent, DNC status, or state law for you - it tells you to check, because it can't know your state, your list source, or your broker's compliance policy.
- It does not send anything itself - drafts route to Slack for review, same as this repo's other content skills.
