When the house is the hardest part to divide

During a divorce, the house is often the biggest shared asset and the hardest to agree on. Who pays for repairs? Who handles showings? What's it really worth? Each question can add weeks and stress.

We're a local, family-run cash buyer. We stay neutral, communicate with both parties, and give one written offer with the comps behind it so everyone is looking at the same numbers.

How we keep it fair and simple

  • Neutral communication. We share the same information with both spouses, and with your attorneys or mediator if you'd like.
  • No repairs to argue over. We buy as-is, so neither side has to pay for or manage work on the house.
  • Privacy. No signs, no online photos and no strangers walking through.
  • A closing date you both agree on. Fast, or timed to your settlement.

Please note: if your divorce is in process, the court or your settlement may control how the house can be sold and how the money is divided. Check with your attorney before you sign anything. We're happy to work with them.

Common questions

Do both spouses need to agree to the sale? +

Generally, if both of you are on title, both need to sign. Your attorney can explain what applies in your case.

How is the money split? +

That's decided by your settlement or the court, not by us. The title company follows the instructions they're given at closing.

Can one spouse still be living in the house? +

Yes. We'll schedule the walkthrough and closing around whoever is living there.

How long does it take? +

You'll have an offer within 24 hours of our walkthrough, and we can close in as little as 7 business days, or on a later date you both choose.
