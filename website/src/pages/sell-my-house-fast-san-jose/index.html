San Jose homes we're glad to buy

A lot of San Jose was built in the decades after World War II, and many of those houses are still owned by the families who first bought them. That's a big part of who calls us. Houses we see often:

  • Original post-war ranch homes with older wiring, galvanized plumbing, single-pane windows or an original roof.
  • Houses with unpermitted work, like a converted garage, an added bedroom, or a backyard unit the city never signed off on.
  • Long-time family homes full of decades of belongings. Take what you want and leave the rest.
  • Rentals with tenants in place. San Jose has its own tenant protection rules on top of state law, which makes selling a rental harder. We can buy with tenants living there.
  • Homes with foundation, sewer line or termite issues that came up in an inspection and scared off another buyer.

Why San Jose sellers choose a cash sale

San Jose buyers tend to expect move-in-ready homes, and getting an older house there can mean months of contractors and a lot of money up front. For some sellers, that work pays off. For others, like an heir living out of state, someone behind on payments, or a family that just needs it done, the cost and stress aren't worth it.

Our honest take: if your San Jose house is already updated and you have time to wait, a traditional sale may bring you more money. We'll tell you that. If you want speed, certainty, and no repairs, a cash offer from us is worth comparing.

San Jose seller questions

Do you buy San Jose houses with tenants still living there? +

Yes. We can buy a rental with the tenants in place, so you don't have to handle move-outs yourself. San Jose has its own tenant protection rules, so we plan the sale around the lease and the tenants' rights.

Will you buy a house with an unpermitted garage conversion or addition? +

Yes. Unpermitted work is common in older San Jose homes. We factor it into our offer, so you don't need to legalize it or tear it out before you sell.

I inherited my parents' house in San Jose. Can I sell before probate is finished? +

It depends on how the estate is set up. Some homes are held in a trust and can sell right away, while others need to go through probate first. We work with your timeline either way, and it's a good idea to talk with an estate attorney about your specific situation.
